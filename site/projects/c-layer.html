Case study Climate tech

C-Layer

A multi-layer robotic system designed to capture CO2 from heavy-industry stacks automatically, without stopping the plant.

  • Self-initiated
  • Sole designer
  • Concept design, no prototype built yet

The problem

Cement plants, steel mills and power stations release large amounts of CO2. Conventional carbon capture systems help, but they bring their own problems:

  • They use a lot of energy.
  • Filters need manual maintenance.
  • Changing filters can mean stopping the plant.
  • They can build up backpressure in the stack, which can damage furnaces and halt production.

The idea

An autonomous filtration unit mounted on the stack. Sensors watch the filters, an AI model predicts when each one will saturate, and a robot swaps it for a fresh one while the plant keeps running.

Four layers

1

Pre-filtration

Removes dust and large particles from the flue gas first.

2

Chemical absorption

MEA bonded to multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) to absorb CO2.

3

Chemical absorption

A second MEA and CNT stage for the remaining CO2.

4

Structural support

Holds the stack against airflow pressure and heat.

How it is designed to work

  1. Sense. CO2, pressure and temperature sensors read the stack continuously.
  2. Predict. An NVIDIA Jetson Nano analyses the readings. An LSTM network estimates the remaining useful life of each filter before it fails.
  3. Swap. When a filter saturates, the robot replaces it. Design target: about 20 seconds, with no shutdown and no human on the stack.
  4. Regenerate. The used filter moves to a chamber where the plant’s own waste heat releases the captured CO2 for reuse.

Engineering choices

Mechanics

NEMA 23 and NEMA 34 stepper motors for torque and precision, linear actuators to push and pull filters, linear guide rails for smooth travel, and a 316L stainless steel frame against heat and corrosion.

Chemistry

Combining MEA with carbon nanotubes is meant to raise absorption efficiency, lower energy use and speed up regeneration. Using waste heat for regeneration targets about 50% less energy than conventional systems.

Safety by design

  • Built for stack environments up to 870°C.
  • Reflective shields designed to reflect about 95% of radiant heat, plus aerogel insulation.
  • Vortex tube cooling keeps the electronics cool with compressed air.
  • A spring-return bypass damper opens on power loss, so the furnace is protected even if the system fails.

AI roadmap

Phase 1

Analyse sensor data and monitor performance.

Phase 2

LSTM models predict the remaining useful life (RUL) of each filter.

Who it is for, and why it matters

Cement plants, steel and iron works, power stations, industrial zones and environmental agencies. The design supports UN Sustainable Development Goals 7 (clean energy), 9 (industry and innovation), 11 (sustainable cities) and 13 (climate action).

Status

C-Layer is a concept design and research project. A physical prototype has not been built yet because it needs funding. The estimated prototype budget is $4,000 to $6,300, covering motors, sensors, the Jetson Nano, the frame and electronics.
